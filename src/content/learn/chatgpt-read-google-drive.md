# Can ChatGPT read files from my Google Drive?

The short answer first, since that is probably why you are here.

On a paid ChatGPT plan, yes. You turn on a connector once, and after that you
can point the assistant at a document sitting in your Drive.

On the free plan, no. There is no connector, and there is no hidden setting
to find. If you have spent twenty minutes looking for the button, it is
because the button is not there.

That is worth saying plainly, because the usual experience is to assume you
missed something. You did not.

The free plan is not stuck, though. Two things work instead, both quick. And
there is a third step that matters more than either of them, which is the
part almost nobody explains.

## You already have a Google Drive

If you have a Gmail address, you have a Drive. It came with the email and it
has been there ever since, whether or not you have ever opened it.

You will find it at [drive.google.com](https://drive.google.com). It is a
folder, like the folders on your computer, except it lives online. Anything
you put in it can be opened from your phone, from your laptop, and by
anything you give permission to.

That is the whole of what you need to know about it here.

## If you pay for ChatGPT

Open the settings and look for connectors. You will be asked to sign in with
Google and to allow ChatGPT access. After that, you can name a document or a
folder in a conversation and it will be read.

Which paid plans include this has changed more than once, and will change
again, so the honest instruction is to look in your own settings rather than
trust any list written months earlier, including this one.

## If you do not pay

**Attach the file to the conversation.** Open Drive, download the document,
and drag it into the chat. The assistant reads it exactly as if it had
fetched it itself. This is the plainest route and it has never depended on a
plan.

**Or use [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com) for that part.** It is Google's
own assistant, it opens with the same account your Drive is on, and there is
nothing to sign up for. A free account can attach files to Gemini and choose
them straight from Drive, without the download step in the middle.

Gemini working inside Drive itself, the version that summarises your
documents from the Drive page, is a paid product and a different thing. The
free account can still hand it a file, which is all this needs.

**Or paste the text.** Unglamorous, always available, and for one page of
notes it is faster than any of the above.

Notice what all three have in common. None of them is a workaround for a
broken thing. They are all the same act: putting the right words in front of
the assistant before you ask the question.

## The step almost nobody explains

Everything above answers whether the assistant can open your file. None of it
answers the question that decides whether any of it helped: which file, and
what is written in it.

This is where most people lose the benefit. They connect the Drive, point at
a document, and get answers no better than before, because the document was
never written to be read by an assistant. It was written for them.

A page of your own notes usually mixes four things together. What is
currently true. What used to be true and was changed. Ideas that were
considered and dropped. Background you understood without writing down.

You can tell those apart at a glance because you were there. An assistant
cannot. It reads the abandoned idea with the same confidence as the decision,
and then recommends the thing you already rejected.

## What to write instead

One page, four answers, in this order:

1. **What is this?** Two lines. Enough that a stranger knows what they are
   looking at.
2. **Where does it stand today?** The current state, not the history.
3. **What has already been decided?** Especially the things you do not want
   revisited.
4. **What happens next?** The one or two things actually waiting.

That is it. It takes about ten minutes the first time and a minute or two
after each session.

The reason this works is not that the assistant becomes cleverer. It is that
you have removed the guessing. Everything on that page is current, because
anything that stopped being true came off it.

Keep one folder for each subject, and keep that page at the top of it. A
folder for the flat you are buying, a folder for the business, a folder for
the course you are studying. When you open a new conversation, the folder is
what you hand over, not the whole history of what you have ever asked.

## The honest limit

Someone has to keep the page current, and that someone is you.

If nobody updates it, it quietly rots. Within a few weeks it describes a
project that no longer exists, and then it is worse than having nothing,
because it looks organised while it lies. An assistant will follow a
confident wrong page straight off a cliff.

The habit that makes it survive is small: at the end of a session that
changed something, change the page. Not a report, just the lines that are no
longer true.
